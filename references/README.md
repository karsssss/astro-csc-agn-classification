## How the three papers relate to each other and to our pipeline

- **Same underlying object, three different levers pulled.** All three papers classify Chandra Source Catalog (CSC) point sources into AGN-adjacent classes using overlapping X-ray features (fluxes, hardness ratios, variability). Yang and Kumaran both add multiwavelength (MW) photometry and both need a labeled training set; Pérez-Díaz uses X-ray properties only and needs no training set. The three form a natural axis: **RF + SMOTE + z-score (Yang) → LightGBM + class-weight + native-NaN (Kumaran) → no classifier at all, just clustering + distance (Pérez-Díaz)**, each step trading away something the previous paper needed (imputation, oversampling, MW data, or labels).
- **Yang vs. Kumaran (both supervised, same problem, different classifier):** Kumaran is the more direct analog to our actual tooling (LightGBM/CatBoost) and demonstrates concretely that native missing-value handling plus class-weighting outperforms Yang's RF-style impute-then-SMOTE approach — this is probably our single most actionable cross-paper takeaway. Yang, in turn, is more methodologically rigorous about *uncertainty*: its Monte-Carlo-sampled Confidence Threshold (CT) has no equivalent in Kumaran (whose CMP is just a raw point probability with fixed percentile cuts). Yang also uniquely applies an SFD-based reddening/absorption correction to AGN training labels — a step neither Kumaran nor Pérez-Díaz do, and one we haven't implemented yet either.
- **Pérez-Díaz vs. the other two (unsupervised vs. supervised):** its GMM-cluster-then-Mahalanobis-label design is structurally similar to the "UMAP → clustering → classifier" shape already sketched in our own `unsupervised.ipynb`, just with a cheaper per-cluster nearest-centroid rule standing in for a trained classifier. Its external validation against Yang's own catalog is the most important cross-paper result: it finds very low precision (0.05–0.42) for its "small accretor" (X-ray binary) class when checked against Yang's MW-informed labels — direct evidence that **X-ray-only features cannot reliably separate AGN from X-ray binaries**, which justifies the MW-feature-heavy approach both Yang and Kumaran (and we) take, rather than undermining it.
- **Net implications for our pipeline** (elaborated further in each paper's note): (1) prefer LightGBM/CatBoost's native NaN handling and class-weighting over imputation + SMOTE, per Kumaran; (2) consider adding an SFD/reddening-based correction for AGN training labels, per Yang, since neither of the other two papers do this and our own notebooks don't yet either; (3) treat X-ray-only clustering (à la Pérez-Díaz, and similar to what `unsupervised.ipynb` already explores) as a useful independent cross-check or anomaly-detection layer, but not a substitute for MW features when the goal is reliable AGN/non-AGN separation; (4) all three papers converge on the same conclusion that severe class imbalance for rare/minority classes is only partially addressed by any of SMOTE, class-weighting, or clustering — worth keeping expectations calibrated for our own silver/unlabeled minority categories.
